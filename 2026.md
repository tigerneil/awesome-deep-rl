# Deep RL frontier guide — 2026

This is a high-signal companion to the historical catalog. It is organized around the parts of RL that now dominate the top AI venues: learning from data, imagining future states, acting as a tool-using agent, coordinating across many agents, and controlling embodied systems under real-world constraints.

> **How to use this guide:** start with the conference map, then follow one canonical baseline, one modern frontier, and one embodied or agentic system. The [interactive homepage](index.html) tracks what you have explored locally.

## Top conference map (2024-2026)

- **NeurIPS** — RL, control, planning, uncertainty-aware decisions, multi-agent RL, world models, scalable benchmarks, and off-policy optimization.
- **ICML** — policy optimization, representation learning for control, model-based RL, and RL with foundation-model priors.
- **ICLR** — robust decision learning, model-based planning, offline RL, safe learning, and sequence models for action.
- **CoRL / RSS / ICRA / IROS** — embodied AI, sim-to-real transfer, dexterous manipulation, locomotion, and safe deployment on real hardware.
- **AAAI / IJCAI / AAMAS / UAI** — planning, Bayesian decision-making, multi-agent coordination, and long-horizon reasoning under uncertainty.

The common pattern across these venues is a shift from single-task policy optimization to general decision policies trained from data, priors, and interaction loops.

## New papers to track (2024-2026)

These are especially useful additions for a current RL homepage and reading list:

- **DeepSeek-R1** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948). A major RL-driven reasoning milestone for LLMs, using explicit reward shaping and reasoning optimization.
- **Open X-Embodiment** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08864) · [dataset](https://robotics-transformer-x.github.io/). A key large-scale dataset and benchmark for cross-robot policy learning.
- **RT-2 / vision-language-action models** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15818). A strong example of decision-making policies grounded in pretrained vision-language models.
- **V-JEPA** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02654). Self-supervised video world models and latent prediction methods that matter for representation learning in control.
- **Eureka** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12931). Reward design from language models, showing how LLMs can help create RL objectives and feedback signals.
- **OpenVLA** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09246). One of the clearest open generalist robot policy stacks in the RL/robotics ecosystem.

## Canonical test-of-time and award-worthy RL work

These papers remain the reference stack even as the field expands into LLMs and embodied systems:

- **Q-learning** — Watkins & Dayan (1992). The foundation for temporal-difference control. [paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00992698)
- **Temporal Difference Learning** — Sutton (1988). The conceptual engine of modern RL. [paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/327200)
- **DQN** — Mnih et al. (2015). The deep RL breakthrough that revived large-scale value-based learning. [paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14236)
- **PPO** — Schulman et al. (2017). The most widely used stable on-policy optimization baseline. [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347)
- **SAC** — Haarnoja et al. (2018). A standard continuous-control baseline with entropy-regularized policy learning. [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01290)
- **AlphaGo / AlphaZero** — Silver et al. (2016, 2017). Landmark self-play and planning work in high-dimensional sequential decision tasks. [paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature16961)
- **MuZero** — Schrittwieser et al. (2020). A foundational model-based RL system for planning with learned dynamics. [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265)
- **World Models** — Ha & Schmidhuber (2018). A classic bridge between latent environment models and control. [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122)
- **Inverse Reinforcement Learning** — Ng & Russell (2000). Still foundational for reward learning and preference-based control. [paper](https://ai.stanford.edu/~ang/papers/icml00-irl.pdf)

## LLM and agentic RL

The strongest modern RL work is increasingly coupled to language, planning, and tool use:

- **RLHF** — Ouyang et al. (2022). A major alignment pattern in which preference learning is used to optimize language models. [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)
- **DPO** — Rafailov et al. (2023). Preference optimization without a separate reward model. [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)
- **GRPO / reasoning RL** — DeepSeek-R1-style reasoning pipelines and GRPO-type optimization show the newest RL-for-reasoning direction. [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948)
- **ReAct** — Yao et al. (2023). A canonical reasoning-and-action pattern for language-agent task execution. [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629) · [code](https://github.com/ysymyth/ReAct)
- **WebArena** — Zhou et al. (2023). A realistic benchmark for long-horizon browser agents and tool use. [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13854) · [code](https://github.com/web-arena-x/webarena)
- **OpenVLA / Octo / RT-2 class systems** — open foundation policies for embodied control and instruction-conditioned decision making. [OpenVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09246) · [Octo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12213)

This is the frontier where “LLM agent” and “RL” meet: the system is tasked with acting over multiple steps, integrating tool feedback, and optimizing a decision policy rather than just generating a single completion.

## Embodied AI and real-world RL

- **DreamerV3** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104) · [code](https://github.com/danijar/dreamerv3). A robust world-model baseline across tasks and domains.
- **TD-MPC2** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828) · [code](https://github.com/nicklashansen/tdmpc2). Latent planning with strong performance on continuous-control tasks.
- **OpenVLA** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09246) · [code](https://github.com/openvla/openvla). Vision-language-action policies for robotics.
- **Octo** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12213) · [code](https://github.com/octo-models/octo). A generalist robot policy tuned for multi-task settings.
- **Xiaomi Robotics-1** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15330). Evidence that embodied RL is increasingly driven by large real-world robot datasets and reusable VLA stacks.
- **CoRL / RSS / ICRA / IROS** — still the main venues for real-world robotics RL, including dexterous manipulation, locomotion, attribute adaptation, and safe deployment.

## Foundation and world models

- **DreamerV3** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104) · [code](https://github.com/danijar/dreamerv3). A strong, general world-model baseline with fixed hyperparameters across domains.
- **TD-MPC2** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828) · [code](https://github.com/nicklashansen/tdmpc2). Latent planning and scalable offline-to-online control for continuous tasks.
- **Genie: Generative Interactive Environments** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15391). A bridge between generative models and interactive environment design.

## Offline, hybrid, and data-centric RL

- **D4RL** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07219) · [benchmark](https://github.com/Farama-Foundation/D4RL). The canonical starting point for offline datasets and evaluation.
- **RLPD: Reinforcement Learning with Prior Data** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02948) · [code](https://github.com/ikostrikov/rlpd). A practical recipe for combining prior data with online interaction.
- **Decision Transformer** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01345) · [code](https://github.com/kzl/decision-transformer). A sequence-modeling view of offline control that remains conceptually important.

## Multi-agent coordination

- **JaxMARL** — [code](https://github.com/FLAIROx/JaxMARL). Fast, reproducible multi-agent training and benchmarking.
- **Learning Bilateral Team Formation in Cooperative Multi-Agent RL** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20039). Dynamic team formation under changing roles and objectives.
- **MAPPO** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01955) · [code](https://github.com/marlbenchmark/on-policy). A strong cooperative MARL baseline.

## Generalization, safety, and evaluation

- **Procgen** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01588) · [code](https://github.com/openai/procgen). A standard procedural benchmark for generalization and memorization resistance.
- **Safety Gym** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01745) · [code](https://github.com/openai/safety-gym). A classic constrained-RL benchmark for safe exploration.
- **Empirical Design in Reinforcement Learning** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01396). A reminder to report robust evaluation protocols, scaling, and uncertainty.

## A practical reading path for 2025-2026

1. Start with **D4RL** and **Procgen** to understand data quality and generalization.
2. Read one classical reference: **DQN**, **PPO**, or **SAC**.
3. Learn one model-based baseline: **DreamerV3** or **TD-MPC2**.
4. Move to embodied policies: **OpenVLA**, **Octo**, or **Xiaomi Robotics-1**.
5. Explore a language-agent benchmark: **ReAct** or **WebArena**.
6. For current RL reasoning, read the latest GRPO / DeepSeek-R1-style materials and then study prior RLHF/DPO work.
7. Before claiming a result, report evaluation, robustness, and safety as carefully as the algorithm itself.
